Optics Bench

Build optical setups and see what they do.

  • Ray optics: objects on a lawn, lenses, apertures and glass prisms. See what an eye and a screen see, ray traced on the GPU.
  • Fourier optics (4f): diffraction, spatial filtering and phase contrast with scalar wave optics.
  • Dispersion: wave packets in dispersive media, phase and group velocity, and thunder and whistlers to listen to.
  • Rainbow: rays in a water drop, why there is a bow, the secondary bow and Alexander's dark band, polarisation.
  • Interferometers: build cavities, Michelson, Mach–Zehnder and Sagnac interferometers on an optical table, with beam splitters, polarisation optics and a laser you can sweep. See the free spectral range and the finesse, and watch the field in a cavity build up. A Michelson as a Fourier-transform spectrometer, white-light fringes, and a scanning Fabry–Pérot.
  • Gratings: a grating spectrometer with source and reference lamps. Calibrate it, and see why its resolution is set by the path difference across the grating: δλ/λ ≥ λ/2Nd. Includes PS03, exercise 11 (slit, double slit and grating).
  • Polarisation: linear, circular and elliptical light as in Saleh & Teich 6.1: the wave along z, the ellipse in the x–y plane and the Poincaré sphere. Add polarisers and λ/2 and λ/4 plates and follow their Jones matrices, as in PS03, exercise 13.
Open Optics Bench How to use it

Each name above links straight to that bench. Needs a browser with WebGPU: Chrome or Edge, Safari 26 or newer, or Firefox with WebGPU. There is also a desktop app.

Stat Phys Bench

Partition functions in five examples, and light in a box: watch particles and modes in contact with a heat bath reach the Boltzmann distribution, next to the formulas.

  • Boxes of fixed energy: up to 20 levels and 1000 particles. Drag the levels and watch the particles follow; the Schottky peak of C.
  • A gas in a box: hard spheres in 1, 2 or 3 dimensions, walls that are a heat bath or insulating. Maxwell–Boltzmann speeds and ⟨E⟩ = d/2 kBT.
  • Two-level atoms: the same gas with a ground and an excited level. Z factorises, and C = 3/2 kB plus a bump.
  • Classical oscillators: masses on springs or guitar strings, up to 10 000 modes: equipartition, ⟨E⟩ = kBT whatever the frequency.
  • Quantum oscillators: the same modes with energy steps ε. They freeze out when kBT is small compared with ε.
  • Light in a box: the modes of a cavity as points in k-space and their density of states. Every classical mode holds kBT (Rayleigh–Jeans and the ultraviolet catastrophe); quantised modes freeze out (Planck, Wien, Stefan–Boltzmann).
